## 1. Executive Summary

It was a legitimate download of the winrar application from its official website, and the site's download mechanism caused it to be flagged as malicious by the SIEM. Investigation confirmed the file, URL, and destination IP were clean across multiple platforms. There was no malware nor proxy involved. However, a follow-up was suggested to ensure absolute transparency.

## 5. Root Cause / Attack Vector

The alert was triggered due to the nature of the website where the executable ends up automatically downloading without the users prompts or click-through confirmations which is common in many different phishing websites.

---

## 6. Verdict & Justification

False Positive. The file and URL were verified as clean across multiple platforms (VirusTotal, AnyRun), and IDS logs showed no activity beyond the download itself. HybridAnalysis flagged the file as malicious, but this was weighted less heavily than the VirusTotal/AnyRun consensus, since AnyRun's interactive sandbox allowed step-by-step behavioral monitoring during execution rather than a single automated verdict. Host-level telemetry was unavailable as the device was offline, so this conclusion rests on network and reputation evidence rather than confirmed endpoint state, hence the recommended passive follow-up below.

## 8. Response & Remediation

- **Immediate Actions:** None
- **Follow-up Actions:** Flag the host for immediate log review once the device comes online to ensure no malicious activity or changes took place during the investigation window.
- **Escalation:** No, but recommend a passive follow-up check once host connectivity is restored.

---

## 9. Lessons Learned

This case encapsulated the need to recheck every piece of evidence and its reputation using different platforms before coming to a concrete conclusion as automated platforms might give false or inconsistent analysis. It is also important to follow-up on the unavailable affected devices even if the apparent verdict is a false positive.
